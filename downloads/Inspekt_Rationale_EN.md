# The decisions behind the work

I approached this exercise as a B2B marketing proposal with a specific objective: help a prospective client understand what Inspekt AI offers, recognise when they might need it and take the next step towards an inspection.

The organising idea is straightforward: **turn inspection images into useful information for assessing a façade and planning its maintenance.** The technology explains how this is achieved; the message needs to make its value to the client clear.

**Understand the product and the buying decision.** I began by studying the assessment materials, the original video and the platform's public documentation. I connected its capabilities — image capture, AI-assisted analysis, location mapping and inspection comparison — with questions a technical decision-maker needs to answer: where the anomaly is, what information is available and what should be reviewed before deciding on an intervention.

This highlighted three challenges that guide the proposal: the volume of images to review, information spread across different records and the difficulty of following the same area of a building over time. Inspekt addresses these through its [analysis platform](https://inspektai.com/defect-analysis-dashboard/) and [inspection comparison](https://inspektai.com/year-on-year-comparison/).

**Read the market to find a relevant angle.** The competitive review focused on value propositions, language, stated audiences and the journey from the website to a sales enquiry. Façade AI emphasises its specialised AI; TÜV SÜD, technical capability and compliance; Aerially, coverage, cost and requesting a quote. Their websites show the arguments they use to help prospects move towards purchase. [Façade AI](https://facadeai.com/), [TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-sg/services/inspection/smart-facade-inspection), [Aerially](https://aerially.ai/).

H3 Zoom was a particularly useful reference for connecting reports, use cases and financial arguments. Its calculator turns potential savings into a discussion of the client's circumstances. [H3 Zoom: comparison and calculator](https://www.h3zoom.ai/comparison).

My conclusion is that Inspekt's opportunity lies in showing clearly how its information helps teams prepare a maintenance decision. Specialisation, 3D models and historical tracking also appear in other offerings; the proposal needs to earn credibility through concrete examples, a clear explanation and evidence of its usefulness.

**Choose an audience and make the next step easy.** The submission prioritises the technical or maintenance lead preparing work on a façade. This is a commercial hypothesis I would validate with sales. It gives the message a recognisable need to address and a clear call to action: request an inspection proposal for a building, sharing its location, the need and the timing.

**Give each asset a commercial role.** The video proposal follows six stages: capture, reveal, locate, understand, compare and act. This sequence turns a technical demonstration into a story the buyer can follow.

The presentation develops the problem, the solution and the value for the client. The article explores a technical question and provides context for assessing the service. LinkedIn and advertising adaptations adjust the opening, format and call to action to each channel. The website brings the assets together and makes it easy to move from explanation to enquiry. English and Spanish maintain the same argument with writing that is natural in each language.

**Use design to explain the product.** The proposed visual direction combines clear typographic hierarchy, large images and a recognisable contrast between pale yellow and charcoal. The aim is to help visitors understand where they are, what they are seeing and why it matters. Animations support this reading experience and respect reduced-motion preferences.

**Visual continuity.** The Sagrada Família imagery and recorded architectural model carry the story from the proposed video into the platform experience. Other product screenshots retain their original context; the dated Bangkok comparison is a separate example.

**Build on the submission during the first 90 days.** I would develop the commercial programme in three phases:

- **Days 1–30: validate and prioritise.** Test needs with sales, product and operations; review available case studies and credentials; complete keyword research and the channel audit, using a comparable sample of posts, publishing frequency and engagement.
- **Days 31–60: activate.** Prepare messaging and pages for the priority segments, sales materials, advertising content and an email sequence. Define in the CRM how an enquiry becomes an opportunity and who owns each follow-up.
- **Days 61–90: optimise.** Review enquiry quality and progress towards proposals and commissioned work. Adjust messages, channels and budget; select events for their ability to generate meetings with target accounts.

The brand development, platform concept and calculator form part of this proposed programme. The calculator would use visible assumptions and validated data to support a commercial conversation. Savings or accuracy figures would need a source, a method and an application context.

I would evaluate the work by its contribution to opportunities that sales can develop: relevant enquiries, proposals issued and inspections commissioned. Views, video completion and downloads would help explain which content attracts interest and where it is lost. **The ambition is to connect a clear explanation of the product with a concrete commercial opportunity.**
